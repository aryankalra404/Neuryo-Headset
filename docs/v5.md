# v5 · 7 Nov arch concept (v5.2: rigid midline spine)

**Status:** concept, to test against v4. v4 (v4.5) stays the build plan until the v5 mock-up passes the frame test below.

**Open it:** [designs/v5/](https://aryankalra404.github.io/Neuryo-Headset/designs/v5/)

**Source:** the "Neuryo headset concept study" doc (7 Nov 2026): competitor research, mechanics check and the design checklist. Electronics, channels, pods, strip and session are unchanged from [v4](v4.md).

## Why
The v4.5 brow module had to grow to about 140 × 65 mm and 29.5 mm off the forehead to hold the 70 × 50 mm board. It looks bulky and comes close to the eyes on small foreheads. v5 keeps the forehead bare apart from the strip.

## Mechanics check (estimates, from the concept study)
- **Pod force:** 1–1.5 N each. Three pods at 1 N push the headset up about 3 N.
- **What holds it down without the hook:** weight about 1.3 N, and the pods clamping on skin that faces only about 20% downward add about 0.8 N. Not enough without relying on friction.
- **Rear hook:** below the inion the skin faces 42–49% downward (measured on this head model), so 3 N on the dial adds about 1.4 N of hold-down. At 1.5 N per pod the hook carries about 5 N, about 7 kPa on a 40 × 20 mm pad.
- **Temple pads** stop the rear band pulling the headset backward.
- **Spine bending:** a 78 mm cantilever at 1 N bends about 0.6 mm in plain 12 × 5 mm PETG (about 2 GPa assumed): under 0.1 N lost at the 0.13 N/mm spring, so no steel strip.

## v5.2 rigid spine (8 Nov): replaces the strap
**Why.** v5.1b held tall pods on a strap, against D34 (a soft strap cannot keep a tall pod upright). The 8 Nov research (Neuryo-Research `07-mechanical/frame-and-fit.md`) found no in-hair headset that does that:
- Every in-hair headset found pairs a stiff structure with compliance at each electrode: OpenBCI Ultracortex (C77), Wearable Sensing DSI-24 (C78), CGX Quick-20r (C79), Bitbrain Hero (C81), and Zeto's patent, where a spring per electrode and rigid support blocks make tip force independent of headset weight (C80).
- The one comfortable strap-style headset used flat gel pads and its strip buckled at the back (ABM B-Alert X10, C82). EGI's patent says straps over the top of the head pop off, slide on hair and press a line of skin (C84). Sponge systems only use soft holders when each pad is held from many directions (EGI net, R-Net; C84, C85).
- Stiff arms whose force grows with head size gave hot spots (C82), which the soft spring avoids.

**What.** The 7 Nov bar comes back as a **spine** with two changes: it is **clipped rigidly** to the arch at Cz (a printed saddle with 2 × M3, no pivot), and each pod's force comes only from its own soft spring (about 0.13 N/mm, 1.1 N at about 8.5 mm, about 13 mm of travel). Straight hole rows, wedge seats and the fill tube with its black band (now at 1.1 N) are as in the bar fix below. Gone: the strap, Y clip and legs, back clip, back legs, sliders, stretch marks and the constant-force spring. Rear band and hook stay (hold-down).

**Fitting.** Pin Fz and Pz at the size number, put the headset on, tighten the rear dial, then slide the arch sliders until the Cz fill tube's black band lines up with its port (1.1 N). Save that slider setting per patient.

**Force (rechecked 8 Nov, rigid clip: `tools/bar_gap.py ... --rigid`).** Worst of Fz and Pz across 300–390 mm nasion–inion and ±8% head shape: +3.1 to −2.9 mm from nominal (a curved spine following this head: +4.1 to −8.3 mm). At a 1 N setting the roundest heads drop to about 0.6 N and the longest compress the spring about 10.4 mm, past the old 10 mm stroke. So the Cz band is set at **1.1 N** (8.5 mm) and the carriages give about **13 mm** of stroke (spine about 29 mm above the scalp, arch about 38 mm over Cz): every pod then sits at about 0.7–1.5 N (5.6–11.4 mm compression) and none bottoms out. About 12 kPa was comfortable for 12 min in one study (L54), so keep about 1 N on a 10 mm tip.

**Check in the frame test:** pods stay upright and in contact when the head turns, Cz stays within 5 mm of the tape's 50% mark, no sore spot.

## Superseded: v5.1 midline strap (7 Nov)
**Why.** The pivot bar only fit this head. Making it work on other heads took straight hole rows, a wedge seat on each carriage (the pods sat about 20° off the bar), a slot for the fill tube and soft springs. Too many fixes for one part.

**What.** One 20 mm elastic strap runs from a front Y clip on the midline (just in front of Fz) over the Fz, Cz and Pz pods to a slider on the rear band. Two thin legs run from the Y clip back along the hair to each side pod's front edge, so nothing crosses the forehead skin. Each pod has a printed cap the strap slides through, with a side fill port whose channel runs into the lid's centre hole. The Cz cap has a 12 mm riser. Fz and Pz slide to the patient's size mark (1–9) printed on the strap, measured from the Cz cap. No springs, carriages, size holes or pivot.

**Why it works.** A strap under tension presses each pod in by tension × the angle it turns over that pod, and those angles hardly change with head size or shape. Per newton of tension, on the shared head scaled to 300–390 mm nasion–inion and stretched ±8% front-to-back ([tools/strap_force.py](../tools/strap_force.py)):

| Pod | N per N of tension |
|---|---|
| Fz | 0.92–0.99 |
| Cz (12 mm riser) | 0.76–0.89 |
| Pz (6 mm riser) | 0.84–0.91 |

At about 1.5 N of tension (stretch marks on the back legs, set by hanging 150 g on a piece of the elastic), every pod gets about 1.1–1.5 N on any head.

**Back end (corrected 7 Nov).** The first v5.1 ran the strap straight from Pz to the rear band under the occiput. That line passes about 12 mm inside the scalp, so the real strap would wrap the back of the head and lose tension to friction, and the first Pz figures were wrong. The strap now ends at a back clip on the scalp behind Pz (150° from the front), with two elastic legs down to printed sliders on the rear band. The rear band itself stays a U under the occiput: that is where the skin faces downward, so the hook holds the headset down. Every pod sits on the scalp and pushes along the scalp normal, so there is no reach problem. Same idea as elastic EEG head straps with a midline strap and electrode position marks (US 5,363,858; US 10,130,278).

**Same position every visit.** Each pod is located from fixed landmarks: the arch from the ears (Cz on the T7–Cz–T8 line, checked against the tape's 50% mark), the strip from the nasion template, Fz and Pz from the saved size mark. For comparison, electrodes in a fitted cap vary by 3–12.7 mm SD between people (Atcherson et al. 2007).

**v5.1b hybrid (7 Nov): non-stretch middle.** Size marks printed on elastic move when it stretches, so Fz and Pz could drift a few millimetres with the tension. The Fz–Cz–Pz section is now 20 mm PP webbing, which does not stretch, with heat-punched size holes the pods pin into (5 per pod, 4 mm apart, two sizes each, positions scaled with nasion–inion, punched through a printed jig). Elastic only at the two ends. Webbing bends freely, so the tension still presses each pod as in the table above; a printed PETG strip would not, because it is stiff enough to act like a beam over the caps. The Cz cap has a loose fork that straddles the arch with about 5 mm of vertical play, so the strip is registered to the ears front-to-back but the arch and the two side pods (about 30 g each) never rest on Cz; their weight goes to the side pads, temple pads and rear hook. Every pod's force comes from the strap tension alone. Same idea as an EEG headset strap with inelastic electrode portions and elastic portions (US 11,583,231).

**Check in the frame test:** the strap stays on the midline when the head turns, and the pins stay in their holes.

## Superseded: suggested strap fix (7 Nov review)
Two weak points in v5.1b: the webbing and woven elastic are fabric in the hair next to saline (D32, D34 ask for non-porous, wipeable reusable parts), and the tension depends on staff setting stretch marks on two legs each visit.
- **Printed TPU 95A band** from the Y clip to behind Pz, 20 × 1.2 mm, size holes printed in. Wipeable. At 1.5 N it stretches under 1 mm end to end (datasheet modulus 9.2–26 MPa), and it bends freely (bending length about 12 mm against about 62 mm for PETG), so the pod forces above still hold.
- **One constant-force spring in the back clip** (stock: 1.4 N, 338 mm travel, 9.67 mm coil): the same tension on every head with no stretch marks, so each pod gets about 1.06–1.39 N (0.76–0.99 N per N). Y legs and back legs become fixed-length TPU.
- Test it beside the webbing strap in the 10-head mock-up. No Indian seller for the spring found yet.

Sources: [Lesjöfors constant force springs](https://shop.lesjofors.com/global/en-US/all-products/flat-springs/constant-force-springs-p-1035/) · [Ultimaker TPU 95A TDS](https://www.utwente.nl/en/rpl/Data%20sheets/Ultimaker%203/TPU%2095A.pdf) · [Bambu TPU 95A TDS](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0645/5876/0155/files/Bambu_TPU_95A_Technical_Data_Sheet.pdf)

## v5.2b parts as built (8 Nov): no parts passing through each other
The model now shows the midline as printable parts that fit together:
- **Spine:** two straight halves (12 × 5 mm PETG) meeting in a 144° V over Cz (each half runs about 18° off the scalp's tangent at Cz and about 20° at Fz and Pz). At each carriage it widens to 18 mm with a 5 mm slot for the fill tube; the 2 mm size holes sit in the right-hand rail, the cables run along the left.
- **Carriages:** one print per pod, a 24.4 mm tube (1.3 mm wall) on the pod's axis with its top cut to the spine's underside (a wedge of about 20° at Fz and Pz, a V at Cz), a 2 mm cap as the spring seat, two snap lips over the 18 mm seat and a 1.75 mm filament pin through the size hole. A notch on the cable side gives the snap lead its 13 mm of travel.
- **Fill tube:** 3.5 mm OD, printed on the pod lid, up through the spring, the cap and the spine's slot. Its black band sits level with the spine's top at 1.1 N, so the spine itself is the force gauge.
- **Arch:** now crosses the midline 27 mm behind Cz (was over Cz), so the Cz fill tube is open from above. A printed clip round the spine and arch (bottom plate, cheeks, a bridge with a 4 mm cable channel, top plate, 4 × M3 × 25 mm) holds them; Cz stays fixed relative to the ears.
- **Forehead strip:** the temple pads now sit on the strip's end tabs instead of through them.

## Bar fix (7 Nov): straight rows and a soft spring (current again in v5.2)
**Problem found.** The bar followed this head's curve at a fixed height, so it only fit this head. On other heads the scalp under the Fz and Pz holes moved closer or further away, and the pivot only evens Fz against Pz. Checked on the shared head's midline profile, scaled to 300–390 mm nasion–inion and stretched front-to-back by ±8% ([tools/bar_gap.py](../tools/bar_gap.py)):

| Bar | Gap error at Fz/Pz (mm), 300 → 390 mm nasion–inion |
|---|---|
| Follows this head (before) | +1.6 → −5.6 for this head's shape; +4.0 to −8.2 across shapes |
| Straight along the Cz–Fz and Cz–Pz lines (now) | +0.7 → −0.4 for this head's shape; +3.0 to −2.8 across shapes |

With the old 0.33 N/mm spring and 6 mm travel, the headset floats until the pods' total push equals the hold-down, so the split between Cz and Fz/Pz moved with the error: at −8 mm (a large, long head) Cz lifted off the scalp and Fz and Pz took everything; at +4 mm Fz and Pz fell to about 0.6 N.

**Fix 1, straight rows.** A head one size up is close to this head scaled about Cz, which moves Fz straight out along the Cz–Fz line. So the bar runs straight along that line through the Fz holes (and along Cz–Pz through the Pz holes). Each hole then sits the same height above its own site, with no extra part and no extra step for staff. The two halves meet in a shallow V at the pivot, about 27 mm above Cz.

**Fix 2, softer spring.** About 0.13 N/mm with about 10 mm of stroke (1 N at about 7.5 mm compression; for example about 6.5 mm across, 0.4 mm wire, 18 mm long, from an assortment and checked on a kitchen scale). The ±3 mm left by head shape then moves each pod by about ±0.25 N instead of about ±0.6 N. The carriages grow 4 mm, so the bar sits about 26 mm above the scalp (was 22) and the arch about 35 mm above Cz (was 31).

**Checked, no change needed:** arch sliders (this head's 355 mm ear-to-ear arc scaled to 500–580 mm heads needs about ±13 mm a side, inside ±15); rear hook pressure (about 6–7 kPa at 5 N on 40 × 20 mm); temple arms (a ±4% longer or shorter head moves the temple pad about 3 mm, inside the 16 × 22 mm pad and the strip's S-bend); size-hole spacing (2 mm of Fz travel per size, holes 4 mm apart, within 2 mm).
- **Arch sliders:** about ±15 mm per side for 500–580 mm heads (this head's 355 mm ear-to-ear arc scaled with circumference).

## Wiring
- Fz, Cz, Pz: along the bar to Cz, then inside the arch down into the left pod (J1).
- M1 and the ear clip: straight up into the left pod (J2, J6).
- M2, battery and the right ECG lead: up into the right pod and over the arch (J3, J5, J7).
- Strip (Fp1, Fpz, Fp2): its left end plugs into the socket under the left temple pad; the eye lead plugs in beside it; both run along the left temple arm (J2, J3). About 12–20 cm.
- **Electrode leads are soldered to the board.** The reference board's vertical input headers (Samtec TSW-102-26: 5.84 mm post on a 2.54 mm body, about 8.4 mm) would not fit the 20.5 mm pod, so they are left off and each lead is soldered into its two holes, glued for strain relief and led out through the pod's underside. The PCB is unchanged; leads still unclip at the electrode end.
- Bias leaves on J2 through the strip's tail. The antenna sits on the pod's outer face, away from the steel arch.

## Forehead strip: keep the hydrogel strip (decided 7 Nov)
Semi-dry forehead pods were considered to make the strip look less like gel pads. Not adopted:
- **Clinical precedent is a frameless adhesive strip.** The three main forehead EEG monitors all use single-use, pre-gelled, peel-and-stick strips with nothing pressing on them: Medtronic BIS Quatro (printed Ag/AgCl, KCl gel in a sponge, foam adhesive; 11 × 1.1 in, 3.7 g), Masimo SedLine (6 gelled electrodes on adhesive tabs) and GE Entropy (3 pre-gelled electrodes). Masimo warns that pressing too hard on its electrodes lowers signal quality and adhesion.
- **Signal:** semi-dry tracks wet gel closely on the forehead but is still a little worse (Zhu 2024: r = 0.98 at Fp2; alpha SNR 13.4 vs 15.3 dB).
- **Frame:** semi-dry pods need a spring, which brings a frame back across the forehead, and saline can run toward the eyes.
- **Looks:** fix with a slimmer printed-flex strip in the BIS style when the supplier makes the product strip.

Sources: [BIS Quatro (Medtronic)](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/healthcare-professionals/products/patient-monitoring/brain-monitoring/brain-sensors/bis-quatro-sensor.html) · [SedLine adult sensor IFU (Masimo)](https://techdocs.masimo.com/globalassets/techdocs/pdf/lab-9850e-eifu.pdf) · [Entropy sensor 510(k) K050835 (FDA)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf5/K050835.pdf) · [Zhu et al. 2024, RSC Advances](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/ra/d4ra05560h)

## Pod fill tube (7 Nov; current again in v5.2)
The top-up port sat about 13 mm above the pod lid, so a drop that missed the 2 mm lid hole landed on the lid and could reach the snap stud and lead. A printed fill tube now rises from the lid hole, through the spring and out of the carriage top (4 mm OD, 2.5 mm bore, part of the lid print). It moves with the pod, so the syringe puts saline straight into the cup. A black band on the tube lines up with the port at 1 N: more tube showing means more force, which gives staff and the frame test a quick force check. Wipe the snap and pod top dry after each session; dried saline corrodes the snap.

## Frame test before electronics
Mock up the arch from the spring band of cheap over-ear headphones. Wear it on 10 heads from 500 to 580 mm, with thick, oiled and short hair, braids, glasses and a hearing aid if possible. Nod and turn for 12 minutes. Include the smallest and largest nasion–inion you can find (sizes 1–2 and 9). Pass if Fz, Cz and Pz each stay between about 0.7 and 1.5 N on a kitchen scale, the bar does not bend, the arch top stays within 5 mm of the size tape's Cz mark and nobody reports a sore spot.

## Ultracortex test rig (8 Nov)
Before printing a v5 spine, test "rigid frame + spring per pod" on real heads in an OpenBCI Ultracortex Mark IV medium frame (48–58 cm). Its midline has nodes at the vertex and about 84 mm (frame surface) in front of and behind it, which land near Fz and Pz on a medium head. Model and print files: [designs/uc-adapter/](../designs/uc-adapter/) (generator: [tools/uc_adapter.py](../tools/uc_adapter.py)).
- **Node ring:** 25.2 × 6.4 mm, glued into the node's 25.4 mm hole instead of OpenBCI's insert; M20 × 2 inside (0.4 mm clearance).
- **Pod tube:** screws into the ring, so the pod reaches 6–28 mm below the ring's inner face (about 15 mm is nominal). 14.8 mm bore, spring seat at the top, 4.6 mm fill hole and 2.4 mm lead hole in the knob, two slots that stop the pod's pins at about 0.55 N and 1.45 N.
- **Pod body:** 14.2 × 11 mm (2 mm taller than v4 so the pins sit in the wall), lid with a 26.5 mm fill tube. Tube top flush with the knob = 1 N; groove showing = 1.5 N.
- **Tip collar:** press-fits into the cup, holds a 10 mm foam tip about 2.5 mm proud.
- The v7.2 pod body (`designs/v7/stl/pod-body-rig.stl`) replaces this pod body for new prints; the node ring and pod tube are unchanged.
- **Buy:** compression spring 8 mm OD, about 29 mm free length, about 0.13 N/mm. Pins: 1.75 mm filament, about 4.4 mm long.
- The Mark IV frame files are OpenBCI's (GPL-3.0); only the node hole size was measured from them, and no OpenBCI geometry is in this repo. Zeto's support-block patent (US 11,166,673) still needs a check before any product, not for this test rig.
