## Why (changes from v4 and v5)
- **Midline on the head, not over it.** A band lying on the head sets each pod's height from the scalp, so the spring only takes up hair. v5's rigid spine stood 29 mm off the head; the gap changed with head shape, which forced a long spring, wedge-cut carriages and an offset arch. Same principle as Zeto's FDA-cleared headset (semi-rigid bands with fixed Fz, Cz, Pz and a spring per electrode: Neuryo-Research C72, C80) and OpenBCI's spring holders (C71, C77). A light strip over the top on a headband was the most comfortable layout in Hairston 2014 (C82).
- **Small front anchor instead of the brow module.** The board is now above the left ear, so the front only has to hold the band's end and set its 0 mm mark over Fpz.
- **Head ring instead of the arch.** A band that lies on the head has to be held at both ends; the arch could not hold its front end. The front band and anchor do.
- **Cam lock above the dial.** v4 clamped the tail on the battery pod at the back; the battery is now above the right ear.

## Checks
- Model: windows and lining are real openings and the lead leaves each carriage through a slot. A collision check of the frame, band, anchor, carriages, pods and leads on the page (8 Nov) found no parts passing through each other; the only overlaps left are leads plugging into their snap heads and the pods.
- Not yet measured: the harness range (PN521), the front band's fit on small and large foreheads, the anchor size, spring force (kitchen scale). Patent: Zeto US 11,166,673 is probably still in force; check its claims before a product.
