**v4.1 frame update (7 Nov 2026):** the soft midline strap, S/M/L frames and knit cover were replaced by a buildable frame: a bought hard-hat ratchet halo, a rigid 3D-printed spine with spring-loaded pod carriages, and a wipe-clean silicone lining (D34). Everything on the frame can be bought online or 3D-printed.

**v4.2 fit update (7 Nov 2026):** the rigid spine is replaced by a flexible midline band that lies on the head, the forehead strip is placed by a nasion template instead of by the frame, and the fit range is 500 to 580 mm to match D19. Why: see [Fit across heads](#fit-across-heads-v42) below.

**v4.3 clinic update (7 Nov 2026):** pods are set by one size number (1 to 5) instead of mm readings, the hair pods use a pre-wetted single-use tip cartridge with a 2 mm top-up hole instead of a 1 ml reservoir, and the contact check tells staff which pod needs drops. The pod is three printed parts (carriage, sealed pod body, tip cartridge) with its cable along the band edge; the ear clip is a printable design; and the test-kit forehead heart sensor moves to its own I²C bus. See [Pods and wetting](#pods-and-wetting-v43) and [Ear clip](#ear-clip-and-heart-sensor-wiring-v43). Codes like D31 are decisions in `00-decisions.md`; codes like K102, C69, R18 or X29 are evidence rows in `evidence-log.csv`.

## What the product is
A 5-minute brain and heart recording taken in clinic at baseline, day 7 and every follow-up visit of a depression treatment course. It tracks change in the same patient against their own baseline and shows it next to PHQ-9 and the medication class. It is a monitoring aid, not a diagnosis (D01, D31). First customers: hospital psychiatry departments and rTMS clinics.

Why sessions and not continuous wear (D31): the early-change evidence is all session-based; clinicians do not want streams of passive data; and head-worn devices lose adherence fast when worn at home.

## Fit across heads (v4.2)

Modelled on the shared head: the 540 mm mannequin's midline, a frame held between the brow (Fpz) and the back of the halo (inion), and the head scaled to 500 and 580 mm, then made 8% taller or flatter for its length. Numbers are how far the scalp under each pod moves relative to a rigid spine of fixed shape (positive = closer).

| Head | Fz (mm) | Cz (mm) | Pz (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 mm, mannequin shape | −4.4 | −6.6 | −6.0 |
| 580 mm, mannequin shape | +4.4 | +6.6 | +6.0 |
| 540 mm, 8% taller for its length | +2.3 | +3.4 | +3.1 |
| 540 mm, 8% flatter for its length | −2.2 | −3.3 | −3.0 |
| 500 mm and flat | −6.5 | −9.7 | −8.8 |
| 580 mm and tall | +6.9 | +10.3 | +9.3 |

- With v4.1's spring (about 0.23 N/mm and 1.4 N at 6 mm, C71), a head 6.6 mm closer bottoms the spring out and one 6.6 mm farther lets the pod lift off. The band lies on the head, so this gap no longer depends on the head.
- The size rows are geometry. The shape rows assume the height-to-length ratio varies up to 8% either way (about 2 SD, judgement); real Indian head shapes may vary more.
- Forehead: Fp1 to Fpz is 5% of circumference, 25.0 mm at 500 mm and 29.0 mm at 580 mm, so one strip with 27 mm centres is off by 2 mm at most, under the 3 mm re-placement error of a fitted cap (P186). Fpz is 10% of nasion to inion above the nasion, 30 to 35 mm.
- Halo: the ratchet fits 52 to 64 cm (C73); Indian adult heads run 499 to 578 mm (P190). A 3 mm lining reaches about 500 mm.

## Pods and wetting (v4.3)

**Pod: three printed parts plus bought ones.**

| Part | Make it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Carriage | Print, PETG, 0.2 mm layers | One piece: housing, two feet that stand on the band edges either side of the pod window, the size-pin tab, and a small side window (about 5 × 6 mm) for the snap lead. The ring on top is the top-up port. No LED: the app shows contact status. |
| Pod body | Print, resin (SLA) or PETG with 4 or more walls | 14 mm wide, 9 mm tall cup with a side slot for the internal lead, and a lid with a 2 mm top-up hole in the middle and a 4 mm snap stud off to one side. After the contact goes in, the lid is epoxied on for good: one sealed part. |
| Tip cartridge | Print collar (PETG or TPU) + PVA foam | 10 mm foam tip pressed into a thin collar, stored soaked in 0.9% saline in a sealed pack, clicks onto the pod body from below. Single-use. Dome and fingered tips to compare. |
| Spring, Ag/AgCl contact, 4 mm stud, snap lead | Buy | Spring sits between the carriage top and the pod lid (about 6 mm travel, 1 to 2 N). Snap lead: FRI TDE-205. |

What changed from the first v4.3 pod: no reservoir (a standing pool drips into hair and can bridge sites, L06); no separate pod cap or socket ring (the carriage housing already covers the pod, and every extra joint can leak); no status LEDs; cup 9 mm instead of 13, so the whole pod stands about 24 mm above the scalp instead of 28.5 and puts less sideways lever on the band.

**Cables from the pods.** The snap lead clips onto the pod's stud, leaves through a small window in the carriage's side with a slack loop (the pod moves about 6 mm on its spring), drops to the band edge outside the carriage feet, and runs forward in printed cable clips into the brow module to J1. Fz and Pz run along the left edge, Cz along the right, so no cable passes under a pod window.

**Top-up.** Drops go through the lid and the contact's centre hole straight into the foam: 2 to 4 drops (0.1 to 0.2 ml) of 0.9% saline, between blocks only, with a dropper or blunt syringe. Tap water works in a pinch (L30); distilled water does not conduct.

Size holes: one size step moves Fz 2 mm, Cz 4 mm and Pz 6 mm, too close for one row of pins. The holes sit just behind each carriage in two staggered rows (sizes 1, 3, 5 and 2, 4), 2.4 mm across, with the number printed beside each; the pin on the carriage's tab drops into the patient's hole.

Build notes: keep saline away from the snap and solder joint; seal the lead slot with epoxy or silicone, not hot glue. All sizes here are model estimates until the first print.

Has it been done before: semi-dry electrodes with a small saline supply are published (Hua 2019 P29, Zhu 2024 L05, Li 2024 L36, Mota 2013 L04) and sold (Brain Products R-Net: Ag/AgCl pellets with exchangeable saltwater sponges in a silicone net). This exact pod has not been tested.

**Will it dry out?** Possibly. Sponges rose in impedance after about 20 min on hairy scalp without rewetting (L37); the tips go on about 8 to 10 min before the session ends, so the margin is roughly 2× but untested in warm rooms or on oiled hair. That is why the top-up hole stays.

**Wetting check (app and firmware).**
- Between blocks: the ADS1299's AC lead-off (31.2 Hz) runs on one pod at a time and gives its impedance; it is in band, so never during recording (D27).
- During recording: rising 50 Hz on a channel, slow drift, extra low-frequency noise or a flat line flag that pod for the next pause.
- Colours (starting points, set in Study 1): green under about 20 kΩ and steady; amber 20 to 40 kΩ or up more than 50% since the first check (top up at the next pause); red over about 40 kΩ (top up now, recheck before the next block).
- Calibrate first: 5, 20, 50 and 100 kΩ resistors in place of an electrode; the app should read each within 10%.
- Log every reading, so Study 1 shows how often and when top-ups are needed.

**Pod drying test (before freezing the pod).** Five or more heads with oiled, thick and short hair, normal room without AC, impedance every 2 min for 20 min. A: v4.1 cup with 1 ml reservoir. B: wet tip only. C: wet tip plus 2 to 4 drops when flagged. Also dome against fingered tip. Pass: under 40 kΩ, no steep rise to 15 min, no wet hair between sites. Ship the simplest that passes (B, then C).

## Forehead contacts and the brow module (v4.3)

- **MVP:** three separate 22 × 22 mm Ag/AgCl snap pads stuck on through the nasion template card (centres 27 mm apart, centre pad at 10% of nasion to inion), with three snap leads to the board: Fp1 to J2, Fp2 to J3, Fpz to the bias input. This is how ECG pads are connected, so there is nothing new to design.
- **Product:** one custom 3-pad strip with a single connector tail, made by a disposable-electrode supplier once the MVP has fixed pad size, spacing and adhesive.
- **Pads first, headset second.** The pads hold themselves on; placing them by template means their position does not depend on where the halo lands.
- **The brow module never presses on them.** Its underside sits 6.5 mm off the skin over the pads (a pocket), and it rests on a soft pad on the upper forehead. A rigid module on gel pads is uncomfortable and moves them with every frown. The board sits higher in the module as a result.
- **Later option:** a soft insert under the module with three floating snap sockets, so the headset clicks onto the pads and needs no forehead leads. Not in the MVP: it adds a part to align and could add motion noise, for no gain in signal.

## Ear clip and heart-sensor wiring (v4.3)

**Buy the sensor, print the clip.** A small MAX30102 breakout (sold widely in India). Ready-made "ear-clip pulse sensors" mostly output only a beat pulse, not the raw waveform needed for pulse-rate variability; hospital ear probes are transmissive and need a different front end.

**Clip to print (black PETG or TPU 95A):**
- The earlobe's free edge is at the bottom, so the hinge sits below the lobe and the jaws close upward over it.
- Outer jaw: a pocket sized to the module you bought, with the sensor window flush with the inner face.
- Inner jaw: black, with a black foam pad, so room light cannot shine through the thin lobe.
- A small torsion spring on the hinge pin gives a light, repeatable squeeze. PPG signal rises with contact pressure up to a point and then falls as the vessels collapse (in a lab phantom the best was about 35 to 48 mmHg), so the spring must hold without pinching.
- A strain-relief boot where the lead leaves the top; the lead runs up in front of the ear into the halo.
- Quickest prototype: glue the module to a screw-adjustable clip-on earring finding; the screw sets the pressure.

**Wiring.** Five wires to J6: 3.3 V, ground, SDA, SCL and the interrupt line (optional, makes the firmware simpler). I²C at 100 kHz over a thin twisted cable, about 30 to 40 cm. Some cheap MAX30102 boards pull SDA and SCL up to 1.8 V instead of 3.3 V and are then not detected: check yours and rewire the pull-ups if needed.

**Fix from v4:** the test-kit forehead MAX30102 cannot share the earlobe sensor's bus, because every MAX30102 answers at the same I²C address (0x57). It moves to the ESP32-S3's second I²C bus (J8). A TCA9548A multiplexer is the alternative.

## Cleaning model (D32, D34, R18)
- **Single-use, binned after every session:** forehead strip, M1/M2 pads, eye pad, three wet tip cartridges.
- **Wiped between patients (low-level disinfection, noncritical item):** everything else: shell, band, carriages, pod bodies, lining, earlobe clip, dial, halo. Nothing to wash.

Turn on **Display → Single-use vs reusable** (or press U) to see this on the model.

### Building the frame, step by step (7 Nov 2026 notes)

**1. Halo and dial from a safety helmet.** Buy a ratchet-type safety helmet and keep only its harness. Tested choice: Karam PN521 ratchet type with plastic cradle, ₹306 incl. GST on [ToolBuy](https://www.toolbuy.com/tp/karam-isi-marked-shelmet-ratchet-type-safety-helmet-with-plastic-cradle-red-pn521) (also on Karam's own store and Amazon.in). Buy two.
- Keep: the black headband and the ratchet knob (the knob sits at the back of the head).
- Remove: the white X-shaped cradle (unclip its 4 legs from the shell; it sits where Fz, Cz and Pz go), the chin strap and the red shell.
- Check on the first one: whether the headband hooks onto the cradle legs or is riveted. If riveted, cut the legs leaving 1 to 2 cm stubs; they make good screw points for the brow module and battery pod.
- In the headset, the midline band does the job the white cradle did: it holds the halo at the right height.

**2. How the printed parts attach.**
- Brow module: slot on its back; the front of the headband slides in and two M3 screws lock it.
- Battery pod: clips over the ratchet housing, with a hole so the knob stays reachable.
- Midline band: front end pins into the top of the brow module; the back end runs through a cam lock on top of the battery pod, so its length is set per patient. A notch under the brow module lines up with the strip's centre mark, which puts the band's 0 mm mark over Fpz.

**3. Spring carriage (one per pod).**
- The band has a window for each pod and, just behind it, five numbered pin holes for sizes 1 to 5 (nasion to inion 304, 314, 324, 334, 344 mm). One step is 2 mm at Fz, 4 mm at Cz and 6 mm at Pz, so the 2.4 mm holes are staggered in two rows (1, 3, 5 and 2, 4). A pin on a short tab from the carriage drops into the patient's hole. The pod passes down through the window.
- Carriage is a printed sleeve; the pod cup slides inside it (about 0.5 mm clearance) with a small flange at its top and a lip at the sleeve bottom so it cannot drop out.
- A bought compression spring (outer diameter about 8 to 10 mm) sits between the sleeve top and the cup, about 6 mm of travel. Starting point: OpenBCI's electrode spring is about 0.23 N/mm, about 1.4 N at 6 mm (C71). Tune with a kitchen scale.
- The snap lead clips onto the pod's stud, leaves the carriage through a small side window and runs along the band edge, with slack for the travel.
- Fit test: print sleeves at 14.4, 14.6 and 14.8 mm inner diameter and keep the one that slides best.

**4. Version 1 is simpler on purpose.** First print a band with fixed holes at Fz, Cz and Pz for your own heads (no slider), with springs and pods in the holes. Print it in two thicknesses and keep the one that follows the head without sagging. Test that the pods stay upright and touch the scalp. Add the five size holes in version 2.

**5. Measure the harness, then update the model.** Send: headband width and thickness at the front, ratchet housing width, height and depth, and the distance from the front of the band to the knob. The 3D model shows the layout correctly but not these exact sizes yet, so the brow module slot, battery pod clip and cam lock are set once these are measured. Also measure the harness fully closed with and without the lining: it must reach 500 mm.

**Order of work:** frame first (a few hundred rupees), electronics on the bench in parallel (no frame needed; paste and tape or the VIT wet cap for first signals), then put the electronics in the frame with dry or paste inserts, then saline pods. The saline pod (no leaks, foam stays wet) is the riskiest part, not the frame. The full parts list with links is in **Details → Build** on the page.

## Still open
- Study 1 decides whether Cz earns its channel, strip vs paste on the forehead, and earlobe vs forehead PPG.
- Head-ECG (M2 minus M1) vs chest ECG: if it matches, the earlobe clip may go in v5.
- Ear clip spring force and jaw size: set on the first print against the module you buy.
- Brow-module shape, board outline and chip positions are model estimates for the product designer.
- Pod spring force, band material and thickness, and total weight: set by the frame test (D34). Pass marks: 1 to 2 N at Fz, Cz and Pz on 10 or more heads spanning 500 to 580 mm with only clamp positions changed; Cz within 5 mm of tape-measured Cz across two operators.
- Harness range with the 3 mm lining (must close to 500 mm).
- Pod drying test (A vs B vs C, dome vs fingered tip) and the wetting thresholds.
- Strip pad size and adhesive to be set with the hydrogel supplier.
- CDSCO class (B vs C) to be confirmed with a regulatory consultant.
